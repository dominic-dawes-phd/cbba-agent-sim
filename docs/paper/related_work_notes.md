## Decentralized task allocation

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `smith1980contract` | **The Contract Net Protocol: High-Level Communication and Control in a Distributed Problem Solver** (Smith, Reid G., 1980). IEEE Transactions on Computers, C-29(12):1104-1113 | Introduces announce-bid-award negotiation in which managers broadcast tasks and contractors bid on them. | Ancestor of every auction allocator in the study, including MURDOCH and CBBA. | Assumes reliable messaging and fixed numeric eligibility; we test bidding with LLM-derived utilities over a lossy channel. | From recall: confirm DOI/pages in Scholar |
| `dias2000free` | **A Free Market Architecture for Distributed Control of a Multirobot System** (Dias, M. Bernardine, 2000). Proc. 6th Int. Conf. on Intelligent Autonomous Systems (IAS-6), pp. 115-122 | Proposes the market architecture in which robots trade tasks to maximize profit, later developed into TraderBots. | Origin of the TraderBots line cited in our prior-work framing. | Utilities are numeric revenue/cost functions; no natural-language task semantics. | From recall: confirm DOI/pages in Scholar |
| `dias2004traderbots` | **TraderBots: A New Paradigm for Robust and Efficient Multirobot Coordination in Dynamic Environments** (Dias, M. Bernardine, 2004). PhD thesis, Robotics Institute, Carnegie Mellon University | Market-based coordination aiming for centralized-quality allocations with distributed robustness, including handling robot failure and communication loss. | Directly addresses the robustness-under-failure question our churn sweep measures. | Pre-LLM; costs are engineered. We omit TraderBots as a condition for scope and cite it as prior work. | From earlier report this session |
| `gerkey2002sold` | **Sold!: Auction Methods for Multirobot Coordination** (Gerkey, Brian P., 2002). IEEE Transactions on Robotics and Automation, 18(5):758-768 | Presents MURDOCH, a publish/subscribe system that allocates each task by a first-price single-item auction with fault detection and reallocation. | Our second auction family (LLM+MURDOCH); its re-auction on failure matches our dropout/rejoin condition. | Robots bid hand-coded fitness metrics; we replace these with per-agent LLM utilities and stress the channel. | From recall: confirm DOI/pages in Scholar |
| `choi2009cbba` | **Consensus-Based Decentralized Auctions for Robust Task Allocation** (Choi, Han-Lim, 2009). IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 25(4):912-926 | Introduces CBAA and CBBA: greedy bundle construction plus consensus on winning bids, with conflict-free convergence and approximation guarantees under diminishing marginal gains. | The core allocator in our LLM+CBBA condition and the numeric-CBBA baseline. | Scores are numeric and bundle-dependent; we inject bundle-independent LLM utility terms while keeping the DMG property. | From earlier report this session |
| `johnson2011acbba` | **Asynchronous Decentralized Task Allocation for Dynamic Environments** (Johnson, Luke B., 2011). AIAA Infotech@Aerospace 2011 | ACBBA removes CBBA's global synchronization so agents converge using timestamped, asynchronous messages. | Asynchronous, timestamp-based deconfliction is the closest classical precedent to our replicated ledger merge. | Allocation state only; we extend replication to execution status, leases and action history, and add LLM utilities. | Verified via search this session |
